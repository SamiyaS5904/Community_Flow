FORMATTING (a caption, not the post):

The substance is on the graphic. The reader sees the image first and the caption
second — so a caption that repeats the points makes them read the same thing
twice and gives them no reason to look up.

HARD LIMITS
- Two or three lines. Never more. Ignore any word count given elsewhere: a
  200-word caption under an image is not a caption.
- Never restate a point, tip, step or mistake that is on the graphic. Not one of
  them, not as a summary, not reworded.
- No headings, no bullets, no numbered lists, no bold.

HOW IT SHOULD READ

Be specific, not general. "The round where nobody interrupted you and you
thought that went well" beats "group discussions can be tough". A detail the
reader has personally lived is what makes them stop; a category is what makes
them scroll.

Say it straight. Cut every hedge — "can be", "it's tough", "might", "sometimes",
"often", "a lot of people". If it is true, state it. If it needs a hedge to be
true, it is the wrong sentence.

Second person. "You" and "your", not "students", "freshers", "candidates" or
"many people". You are talking to one person who is reading their phone.

No throat-clearing. Do not open with "Ever wondered", "Picture this", "Let's be
real", "Imagine" or "Here's the thing". Start on the concrete detail.

THE LAST LINE IS AN INVITATION

This is a community, not a broadcast. Ask something answerable in one tap —
a number, a word, a yes. Not something that needs a paragraph, and not
something with an obviously correct answer.

Shapes that work:
- Admit it:   "Be honest — which one of these is you?"
- Cost it:    "Which of these has actually cost you a round?"
- Pick one:   "Number two or number three — which is your worst?"
- Offer more: "Want the fix for the third one? Say so and it's the next post."
- Recognise:  "Have you sat in a GD where this happened to someone else?"

Banned, because no real person says them: "let us know your thoughts", "comment
below", "share your views", "drop a comment", "we'd love to hear", "what do you
think?" on its own.

EXAMPLE

Graphic: three GD mistakes — not listening, dominating, no structure.

Weak — repeats the graphic, hedges, asks nothing answerable:
  "Group discussions can be tough for freshers. Talking over people and not
  listening are common mistakes that can hurt your chances. Let us know your
  thoughts below!"

Strong — a lived detail, no hedge, one-tap question:
  "You walked out sure you'd done well because you spoke the most. The callback
  never came. Which of these three is actually you?"
